## 0. Summary

**Where things stand.** Every experiment of the plan (E1–E14) has run: 2 385 repeat×method records over 52 presets (including 5 unused full-protocol Child records), with no failed task. §1 lists each item with caveats. The theory and writing items T1–T4, W3, W4, W6 and W8 are committed on a **local** `experiments/chris` branch of the paper repository. That repository's remote is Overleaf, whose git bridge does not accept branches, so nothing was pushed there (see §6).

**What the experiments show.**

1. **Chunk design works as the new theory predicts (E2, Remark `rem:chunkdesign`).**
   - The conformal step needs only N = 9 chunks at α = 0.1. With 9 chunks of 177 samples instead of 20 chunks of 40, the threshold τ drops from 0.109 to 0.064 (PC). The admitted fraction of candidates drops from 0.87 to 0.59 (PC), from 0.53 to 0.37 (GES) and from 0.995 to 0.77 (FCI).
   - Held-out exceedance stays near α, and the true graph is still retained in 100% of repeats.
   - The "symmetric" design (n_train = n_c = 320) is more selective, but loses the true graph (retained in 55–60% for PC/FCI), as Theorem 1 predicts.
2. **Calibration holds throughout.** Held-out exceedance at α = 0.1 is within about 1.5 SE of α for every method, design and sample size from n = 200 to 6400 (main experiment: PC 0.133 ± 0.029, GES 0.043 ± 0.014, FCI 0.113 ± 0.021). Held-out scores have mean rank 0.50 among the calibration scores (PC), so there is no shift.
3. **The headline experiment (E4) supports the plan's claim, with clear limits.**
   - **Where it works.** When the full-variable run is wrong in a way that depends on which variables are analysed together, CSCD rejects the wrong full-data graph and bootstrap compatibility does not:
     - hidden hubs: PC 90% vs 0%, GES 87% vs 0%, FCI 33% vs 0%;
     - GES at p = 50, n = 1000: 89% vs 0%.
     The AUROC against the well-specified control is 0.95 (PC, hubs), 0.91 (GES, hubs) and 0.94 (GES, p = 50), against 0.61, 0.13 and 0.37 for bootstrap compatibility.
   - **Where it doesn't.** A bias shared by every subset (quadratic edges tested with Fisher-z) is invisible, exactly as Proposition `prop:detect`(b) predicts. Wrong hard knowledge is only weakly detected (10–20%).
   - **False flags.** On the well-specified control, the full-data graph is flagged in 7% (PC), 17% (GES) and 3% (FCI) of runs.
4. **The claim filter does not beat resampling at matched recall (E1, E3). This is the negative result the plan anticipated.**
   - At adjacency level, accepted claims are 98–99.5% precise for PC and FCI (Proposition 4). That is on par with bootstrap/stability selection and clearly above order MCMC (0.90), but no better than voting over the unfiltered candidate pool.
   - At the level of directed claims, stability selection, bootstrap frequency and order MCMC are all more precise at equal recall. For GES the filter even *lowers* average precision (0.67 vs 0.90 for the pool): it removes the sparse, conservative grid candidates that make the vote strict.
5. **Downstream estimation (E11, redone with local IDA on held-out data).**
   - Averaging over the calibrated set lowers the absolute bias slightly against the single run (paired difference PC −0.019 ± 0.008, FCI −0.022 ± 0.014, GES −0.006 ± 0.011).
   - The range of set estimates covers the true effect far more often: 0.63 vs 0.23 for PC, 0.63 vs 0.26 for GES, 0.73 vs 0.54 for FCI.
   - Bootstrap averaging is as good or better for PC.

**Recommended positioning for the paper.** The contribution is a *calibrated self-compatibility test*: finite-sample-valid, with a chunk design derived from the theory, and able to flag errors that resampling cannot see. It is not a better edge-claim filter. The abstract in §5 reflects this; its fallback sentences are no longer needed, because E4 came out positive.
